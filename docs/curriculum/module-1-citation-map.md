## Citation-ready module introduction

> Anatomical terminology provides a shared vocabulary for describing the structures, orientation, and relative positions of an organism's body parts. Bilateral animals have left and right sides, and anatomical planes and directional terms allow structures to be described consistently (Clark et al., 2018). In fishes, observable features such as the jaws, nostrils, operculum, fins, lateral line, scales, and caudal peduncle are commonly used in morphological descriptions and identification guides (Anam & Mostarda, 2012; Garr et al., 2024).
>
> Most fishes examined in this activity exhibit bilateral symmetry. A median or sagittal plane separates left and right portions; a transverse plane separates cranial and caudal portions; and a dorsal plane separates dorsal and ventral portions. Directional terms such as anterior or cranial, posterior or caudal, dorsal, and ventral describe one structure relative to another (Clark et al., 2018). The instructor should explain that plane terminology can differ among human, veterinary, and comparative-anatomy conventions and should use one convention consistently throughout the activity.

## Learning objectives with citations

### Objective 1 — Taxonomic identification

> Identify selected marine vertebrates to an appropriate taxonomic level using observable morphological characters and an instructor-approved identification key (Anam & Mostarda, 2012).

For fishes, students may use FishBase as a discovery and cross-checking resource, but the citation instructions on FishBase recommend crediting the original **Main Ref.** or **Data Ref.** for the specific information used, in addition to citing FishBase (Froese & Pauly, 2026). Accepted marine-species names may be checked through WoRMS, whose individual taxon pages provide their own citations (WoRMS Editorial Board, 2026).

### Objective 2 — External morphology

> Identify and label major external morphological structures of representative marine vertebrates, including structures appropriate to the selected taxon (Anam & Mostarda, 2012; Garr et al., 2024).

The present procedure lists fish-specific structures such as the operculum, scales, and named fins. The objective should not imply that these structures occur in every marine vertebrate. If the chosen specimen is a turtle, mammal, elasmobranch, or another non-bony-fish vertebrate, the instructor must provide a taxon-specific structure list and identification reference.

### Objective 3 — Symmetry, planes, and directions

> Describe the specimen's body symmetry and apply anatomical planes and directional terms consistently when identifying the positions of structures (Clark et al., 2018).

## Procedure with citation placement

1. **Draw two selected marine vertebrates.** Record the specimen source or image source, common name, local name, accepted scientific name, and the identification reference used. Cite the taxon record or identification key beside each scientific name.
2. **Mark symmetry, planes, and directional terms.** Cite **(Clark et al., 2018)** in the instruction or accompanying terminology panel.
3. **Label external structures.** For a bony fish, cite **(Anam & Mostarda, 2012; Garr et al., 2024)** beside the annotated figure or interactive hotspot set.
4. **Measure morphological features.** Cite **(Anam & Mostarda, 2012; Meador et al., 1993)** for TL, FL, SL, and the broader measurement set.
5. **Record presence, absence, or counts.** Define the notation and cite the identification guide governing the count. A check mark, X, or count is meaningful only if the feature and counting method have been defined.

## Required corrections to the supplied measurement table

The table currently uses abbreviations without a legend. Before student release, add a glossary for at least:

- TL — total length
- FL — fork length
- SL — standard length
- PAL — preanal length
- HL — head length
- BD — body depth
- ED — eye diameter
- UJL — upper-jaw length
- LJL — lower-jaw length
- PDL — predorsal length
- IDS — interdorsal space
- 1st DF BL — first dorsal-fin base length
- 2nd DF BL — second dorsal-fin base length
- AF BL — anal-fin base length
- P1 BL — pectoral-fin base length
- P2 BL — pelvic-fin base length

The instructor should confirm this expansion against the original worksheet author or source. In particular, abbreviations such as PAL, P1, and P2 can vary across manuals. Do not let AquaDissect silently assign a meaning without that confirmation.

Other required improvements:

- State the unit once and use it consistently. The supplied table specifies millimetres.
- Define the exact start and end landmarks for every measurement.
- Add an illustration for TL, FL, SL, head length, body depth, and fin-base measurements.
- State how the caudal fin is positioned during TL measurement.
- Separate measurable dimensions from categorical traits such as scale type, caudal-fin type, mouth position, and pelvic-fin position.
- Specify the fin-count convention and provide an example key.
- Include a field for the identification source and taxonomic-record URL.
- Record whether measurements came from a physical specimen, photograph, video, or virtual model; measurements from an uncalibrated image must not be presented as physical dimensions.

## Guide-question citation guidance

Student answers should be evidence-based rather than copied from a model answer:

1. **Importance of symmetry, planes, and directional terms:** cite **Clark et al. (2018)** and connect the terminology to unambiguous observation and communication.
2. **Using external morphology for identification:** cite **Anam and Mostarda (2012)** and the specimen-specific identification key. Students should state which observed characters support their conclusion.
3. **Professional application:** students should cite a source appropriate to the example they choose, such as field identification, fisheries sampling, anatomy, conservation monitoring, or veterinary work. This reflective question does not need one predetermined answer.

## AquaDissect implementation status

The current Tilapia package contains a guided virtual-dissection lesson, but it does not identify this syllabus material as a distinct **Module 1**. A proper Module 1 implementation should therefore be added as a new lesson rather than merely renaming the existing dissection:

1. terminology and body-plan orientation;
2. interactive symmetry and anatomical-plane overlays;
3. external-morphology hotspots;
4. morphometric measurement practice with calibrated imagery;
5. guided identification using observable characters;
6. a formative knowledge check; and
7. claim-level citation markers linked to the references above.

Because Tilapia package 1.0.0 is already published, this addition belongs in a new immutable version after instructor review. The reference PDFs and poster should remain read-only source materials rather than being silently repackaged without attribution and licence review.
