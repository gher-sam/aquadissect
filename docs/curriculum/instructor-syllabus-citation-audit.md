## Citation-ready syllabus wording

The following text can be inserted into the syllabus without changing the intended activities.

### Laboratory orientation

> Laboratory policies, student responsibilities, safety expectations, assessment procedures, and institutional conduct requirements will follow the current Southern Leyte State University Student Handbook and Faculty Manual (Southern Leyte State University, **year to be confirmed**).

**Verification still required:** record the title-page year, revision or edition, issuing office, and stable institutional URL for both manuals. Do not invent a year from the date printed on the course form.

### Exercise 1 — Marine vertebrate body plans and morphological identification

> Students will use observable morphological characters to describe and identify marine vertebrates, apply bilateral-symmetry and anatomical-direction terminology, and distinguish common anatomical planes (Clark et al., 2018). Fish external structures and their functions will be examined using a published fish-anatomy guide (Garr et al., 2024). Total length and standard length will be measured using consistent endpoints described in the U.S. Geological Survey fish-sampling protocol (Meador et al., 1993).

Use the sources beside the relevant claim rather than placing one citation only at the end of the entire exercise:

- Body symmetry, anterior/posterior, dorsal/ventral, and anatomical planes: **(Clark et al., 2018)**.
- External structures, including mouth, nostrils, eyes, operculum, scales, and fins: **(Garr et al., 2024)**.
- Total length, fork length, standard length, and repeatable morphometric measurement: **(Meador et al., 1993)**. The USGS source explicitly defines total and standard length; fork length and any additional measurements in the course table should also be tied to the instructor's chosen fisheries manual.
- Taxonomic identification using morphological keys: cite the **specific identification key or field guide actually used for each specimen**, because a general zoology textbook does not validate a species-level identification.

### Exercise 2 — Sampling methods used to study marine vertebrates

> Students will compare non-invasive field methods used to observe marine vertebrates, including standardized survey routes or transects, visual observation, photo-identification, location recording, group counts, behavior notes, and environmental observations. NOAA Fisheries describes line-transect surveys conducted along pre-established tracklines and the collection of species, abundance, location, behavior, and environmental data during marine-mammal surveys (National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration Fisheries, n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

Use:

- Survey design, tracklines, sightings, counts, species identification, and environmental observations: **(National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration Fisheries, n.d.-a)**.
- Photo-identification, distinguishing marks, GPS location, behavior, group size, and ethical/permit safeguards: **(National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration Fisheries, n.d.-b)**.
- If the exercise teaches capture, tagging, tissue sampling, drones, acoustics, or animal handling, add the **specific permit, animal-care, and method protocol** used by the institution. The two NOAA web pages do not authorize local capture or invasive handling.

### Exercise 3 — Animal classification and cladogram construction

> Students will compare characters, distinguish shared ancestral and shared derived traits, and use the resulting evidence to construct and interpret a cladogram. Phylogenetic hypotheses can be developed from morphological and molecular evidence, while shared derived characters help define relationships among taxa (Clark et al., 2018).

Use **(Clark et al., 2018)** beside the explanations of phylogeny, homologous characters, ancestral versus derived traits, and cladogram interpretation. Cite the original dataset or taxonomic source separately if students are given real species-character data.

### Exercise 4 — Fish necropsy

> Students will conduct a systematic, instructor-supervised external and internal examination of a fish specimen. The examination begins with specimen history, measurements, photographs, and inspection of external surfaces before the body cavity is opened and internal organs are examined and documented (Meyers, 2006). A fish-anatomy guide will support recognition of major external structures and internal organs and their functions (Garr et al., 2024).

Use:

- Systematic necropsy sequence, external examination first, internal examination, sample handling, findings, and reporting: **(Meyers, 2006)**.
- External structures and internal organs such as the gills, liver, gallbladder, spleen, kidney, stomach, intestine, gonads, pyloric caeca, and swim bladder: **(Garr et al., 2024)**.
- The supplied exercise's rabbitfish, parrotfish, tuna, and scad identification should cite a **species-level regional field guide or taxonomic key**.
- Biological and sharps safety must follow the institution's current laboratory safety and waste-disposal procedures. Add the exact institutional manual and local biosafety rules used in the laboratory.

### Exercise 5 — Fish skeleton mount

> Students will identify major osteological regions and relate skeletal features to fish form and function. A published marine-fish osteology guide may be used for terminology, comparative identification, and specimen-preparation context (Cannon, 2017).

Use **(Cannon, 2017)** for osteological terminology and comparative skeletal identification. This citation does **not** by itself establish that every preparation step or chemical named in a local mounting procedure is safe. Before final release, the instructor should attach:

1. the exact institution-approved skeletal-preparation protocol;
2. safety data sheets for every chemical or biological agent used;
3. personal protective equipment, ventilation, sterilization, and disposal requirements; and
4. a specimen-acquisition and wildlife-compliance statement.

### Exercise 6 — E-Turtle School

> Students will study sea-turtle biology, anatomy, species identification, threats, injuries, and conservation through the Olive Ridley Project's E-Turtle School (Olive Ridley Project, n.d.). NOAA's sea-turtle anatomy manual provides reference material for species identification, measurements, dissection, and anatomical systems (Wyneken, 2001), while necropsy procedure should be sourced from a dedicated necropsy manual (Wolke & George, 1981).

Use:

- Online learning modules on sea-turtle biology and conservation: **(Olive Ridley Project, n.d.)**.
- Anatomy, identification, measurements, and dissection reference: **(Wyneken, 2001)**.
- Necropsy procedure: **(Wolke & George, 1981)**.

**Important distinction:** Wyneken (2001) describes itself as an anatomical reference rather than a complete necropsy guide. It should not replace the dedicated necropsy protocol.

### Exercise 7 — Marine-turtle monitoring using citizen science

> Students will examine how citizen scientists can contribute standardized observations of marine turtles by recording identifying marks, date, time, location, and photographs while maintaining a safe distance from the animal (National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration Fisheries, n.d.-c). The activity may also connect observation data to spatial conservation planning (Aiuti et al., 2026) and to documented threats such as petroleum exposure (Ruberg et al., 2021).

Use:

- Citizen-science observation fields, photographs, identification, and safe-distance guidance: **(National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration Fisheries, n.d.-c)**.
- Spatially informed conservation and monitoring context: **(Aiuti et al., 2026)**.
- Petroleum toxicity as a marine-reptile threat: **(Ruberg et al., 2021)**.
- Add the exact local program protocol if the exercise submits data to a Philippine database or partner organization. NOAA's Honu Count is a Hawaii program and should be presented as a credible model, not as the local reporting destination.

## Corrections required in the photographed bibliography

1. **“Miller's and Harley, J. Zoology, 18th Edition, 2010” is bibliographically incorrect.** The 2010 book is *Zoology* (8th ed.) by Stephen A. Miller and John P. Harley. The “18th” should not be retained.
2. **“Chamero, C.” appears to be a transcription error.** A library catalogue records Christine R. Chanco's *General Zoology: Lecture Manual* with a copyright date of 2008. The photographed syllabus appears to show 2005. Confirm the actual title and copyright pages before citing it.
3. **Jenkins is an editor, not the sole author.** Cite the book as an edited volume: Jenkins, S. (Ed.).
4. **The Roy/Paramanshu entry is not sufficiently verifiable as written.** The photographed line should not be converted into a confident APA citation until its title page, copyright page, ISBN, and publisher spelling are inspected.
5. **Web addresses are not separate numbered references.** Each URL or DOI belongs at the end of its corresponding reference entry.
6. **Aiuti et al. needs the complete author list, article number, and DOI.** The verified Frontiers record is included below.
7. **Ruberg et al. needs its journal volume, issue, pages, and DOI.** The complete record is included below.
8. **The Student Handbook and Faculty Manual need exact institutional metadata.** Record the corporate author, year, full title, edition/revision, and stable URL.
9. **Internal SLSU exercise manuals need document metadata.** List the named author or responsible department, year, exact title, version, institution, and repository or course location. If unpublished, identify them as unpublished course materials rather than presenting them as externally peer-reviewed sources.

## Application-integration recommendation

AquaDissect should store a stable source identifier on each lesson step, anatomical description, hotspot, safety notice, and quiz explanation, rather than showing only one undifferentiated bibliography for the whole species package. A minimal content pattern is:

```json
{
  "text": "Total length is measured from the closed mouth to the extreme tip of the tail.",
  "citations": [
    {
      "source_id": "meador-1993-usgs-ofr-93-104",
      "locator": "Fish processing and measurements section"
    }
  ]
}
```

The package manifest can then contain the full source record once, keyed by `source_id`. The interface should display citation markers beside the supported statement and provide the complete reference in a References panel.

Because the existing Tilapia package version 1.0.0 is published, it should remain immutable. Citation integration should be released as a new package version, such as **1.0.1**, after the instructor approves this source map and supplies the unresolved institutional and local-protocol references.

## Publication decision

The syllabus can be described as **substantially source-mapped but not yet fully cited**. Exercises 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 7 have credible sources ready for use. Exercise 5 and the institutional orientation section still require exact authoritative documents. Full citation status should be claimed only after those gaps are resolved and every reused image, table, diagram, and assessment item has its own attribution or licence record.
